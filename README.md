# R-A-I-N-B-O-W-SRAINBOW
by Nathan Andrew Thomas
Composed April 7, 2024 | Acoustic guitar
01 / THE IDEA
I composed Rainbow around the letters of a single word: R-A-I-N-B-O-W. I am not
simply singing a word or setting its syllables to a melody. I treat each letter as
its own vocal event, using its sound and spoken name as material for the music.
I composed the piece on an acoustic guitar on April 7, 2024. I built it around two
chords that alternate. They provide the harmonic framework while I extend letter
sounds, surround them with vowels, repeat them, and shape them into phrases. The
melody develops through the way I sing the letters.
02 / THE LETTER AS A VOCAL EVENT
For example, one of my phrases is:
“ooooh R ooooo R”
I am not just pronouncing the letter R. I create a gesture around it: a sustained
vowel, an arrival at the letter sound, and a repetition. Those actions become part of
the musical material.
I use a related approach with other letters, as in “Aaaaaa of A,” “I I…,” and “ooooh
oh N oh N.” Each letter offers different sounds, and those differences give me ways
to change the vocal line without abandoning the underlying musical pattern.
03 / ARTISTIC STRUCTURE
What interests me is the tension between repetition and variation. The two chords
keep returning, so the accompaniment establishes something familiar and predictable.
The voice changes because each letter has a different phonetic character.

I am exploring the point where language and melody influence one another. Sometimes
the sound of a letter suggests the phrase; sometimes I shape the letter to fit a
musical gesture. The letters are not conventional lyrics laid over the music. They
are the building blocks of the melody.
04 / HARMONY
Technically, the two alternating chords can be described as a two-chord vamp, or a
harmonic ostinato if the pattern repeats consistently. This repeating framework
supports the vocal line and makes the variations in the letter-based phrases more
noticeable.
05 / PHONETICS AND VOCAL COMPOSITION
I work with three interacting layers: harmony, phonetics, and vocal composition.
Harmony is the alternating two-chord foundation. Phonetics is the material I draw
from the sounds and syllables associated with each letter. Vocal composition is what
I do with that material: sustain vowels, articulate consonants, repeat sounds,
introduce pauses, and move from one gesture to the next.
This approach relates to vocalise and experimental vocal music. I would describe my
particular method as a letter-based vocal composition over a two-chord harmonic vamp.
06 / REPETITION AND FORM
The repeating chords give the piece continuity. As I move through R-A-I-N-B-O-W, each
letter creates another opportunity to change the phrasing, duration, articulation,
and melodic shape. The word establishes a sequence, but the vocal treatment gives
that sequence its musical character.
07 / WHAT THE WORK EXPLORES
RAINBOW is my exploration of how the names and sounds of letters can be stretched,
repeated, and shaped into music. I use a limited harmonic framework so the voice can
focus attention on small changes in sound. The familiar act of spelling a word
becomes a way of composing and listening.
